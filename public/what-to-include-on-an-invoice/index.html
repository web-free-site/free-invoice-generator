What to include on an invoice

A clear invoice is easier to pay and easier to trace later. The exact legal fields depend on your country, but most small invoices share the same core list. Use this checklist when you fill Free Invoice Generator.

Steps

  1. Identify both parties. Include your business name and address, and the client's name and billing address when you have them.
  2. Give the invoice an identity. Add a unique invoice number, the invoice date, and the payment due date or payment terms.
  3. List what was supplied. Each line should describe the work or item, the quantity, and the unit price. Vague lines such as "services" slow payment down.
  4. Show the arithmetic. Subtotal, discounts, tax if applicable, and the amount due should be visible. State the currency.
  5. Say how to pay. Add the payment method you actually accept (bank transfer details, mobile-money number, or other instructions) and any purchase order or reference the client gave you.

Optional but useful lines

A short thank-you note, late-payment terms if you use them, and a contact email help. Do not put secrets or card numbers on the PDF.

Quotes versus invoices

A quote is an offer; an invoice is a request for payment after (or at) the sale. If the client has only approved an estimate, send a quote first, then an invoice when the work is billable.
